System Architecture & Detection Principle

Commercial agricultural pesticides frequently formulate active molecules (such as organophosphates, pyrethroids, and carbamates) in hydrocarbon solvents, emulsifiers, wetting agents, and amine-stabilized carriers. When applied to produce peels, residues produce two distinct physical manifestations:

1. Volatile Headspace Degassing: Volatilization of organic solvents, aliphatic hydrocarbons, and organonitrogen decomposition compounds detected by the MQ-2 (hydrocarbon/alcohol sensitive) and MQ-135 (air quality, VOC, and amine sensitive) semiconductor gas sensor array through an ADS1115 16-bit ADC.
2. Optical Surface Morphology: Dried droplet rings, chalky pesticide powder coatings (mancozeb, kaolin), copper fungicide stains (Bordeaux mixture), and sulfur dust detected via the ESP32-CAM high-resolution optical inspection and computer vision feature extraction pipeline.

Scientific Limitations & Regulatory Notice

Indicative Screening Tool: Semiconductor gas sensors (MQ-2 and MQ-135) detect broad classes of volatile organic compounds, solvent carriers, and ammonia/amine derivatives rather than isolating individual pesticide molecules. High natural ripening emissions (such as excessive ethylene or terpene release from overripe fruit) may modulate headspace readings. This system is designed as an accessible, rapid point-of-sale and kitchen screening tool to empower consumers and organic farmers, and does not replace certified chromatographic laboratory verification for statutory regulatory compliance.
